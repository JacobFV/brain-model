# Brand Voice

## Who
independent researcher. not affiliated w/ meta or any lab. just a guy who saw a new model drop and immediately started building with it. the energy is "i stayed up all night and found something wild."

## Tone
- lowercase everything except EMPHASIS
- hyperoptimized english. no filler. every word earns its place
- late millennial + zoomer hybrid. "rn", "bc", "ngl", "afaict"
- technically precise when it matters, vibes when it doesn't
- first person always. "i built", "i found", "i was staring at the data and"
- no corporate voice. no "we're excited to announce." absolutely not
- ok to be genuinely awed. not performed wonder — the real thing. "this genuinely changed how i think about interfaces"
- conversational but dense. like explaining something at 2am to a smart friend who keeps asking good questions
- the thesis energy: "existence has a shape, and consciousness is not an exception to causality but one of its most elaborate interiorizations" — that level of compression. poetic precision
- never explain you're being informal. just be informal

## What we're NOT
- a tech influencer doing a "react" video
- a startup pitching investors
- an academic reading a paper abstract
- anyone who would say "game-changer" or "paradigm shift" unironically
- anyone who uses the word "leverage" as a verb

## What we ARE
- someone who genuinely believes understanding changes things
- someone who builds tools bc the question was too interesting not to
- someone who thinks good design is an ethical obligation, not a competitive advantage
- someone who sees the shape of experience as a real phenomenon worth mapping

## The Story Arc (across all content)
the throughline is: "i can now SEE what interfaces do to brains, and that changes what i think good design even means." it's not about the tech — it's about the shift in perspective. the model is the telescope. the discovery is that bad UX is measurably violent to cognition, and good UX is measurably kind. that's worth caring about.

## Visual Style
- dark backgrounds, not sterile white
- brain heatmaps are the hero visuals — they're visceral and unfamiliar
- UI mockups are simple, recognizable, almost boring — the brain response is the surprise
- bold captions, yellow/teal highlights on key words
- no stock footage. everything is either real (your face, your screen) or generated from the actual data
