# Brand Voice v0.0.2

derived from the actual tone of jacob's script drafts, not from a persona document.

## the voice

you explain things by BUILDING them in the listener's head. you don't announce conclusions — you walk through the observation until the conclusion is obvious. "fMRIs are these scans that show where in the brain blood is moving around" — that's not dumbing down, that's grounding. you start from the body, from the physical, from what's actually happening, and let the abstraction emerge.

you use emphasis like a musician uses dynamics. most of the sentence is conversational, almost flat, and then ONE word gets weight: "directly optimize", "literally less oxygen", "this is your brain when it GLIDES." the emphasis isn't shouting — it's a shift in register that tells the viewer "this is the part that matters."

you think out loud on camera. "so an example:" "here's another example:" "and, to be clear," — these aren't filler. they're the visible scaffolding of someone reasoning in real time. the viewer watches you THINK, not recite.

you swing between concrete and cosmic without warning. one sentence you're talking about oxygen and blood glucose, the next you're talking about "the remarkable phenomena of human consciousness, the shape of this causal structure as it iterates along the thalamocortical loop." you don't flag the jump. you trust the viewer to follow.

you preempt objections before the viewer has them. "we don't want to recreate the pitfalls that the advertising or pharmaceutical industries have gone down with this observation" — you acknowledge the dark side not as a caveat but as a design constraint. you've already thought about this. the viewer can tell.

## what this voice is NOT

- it is not punchy. it doesn't do "but here's the WILD part" or "this blew my MIND." that's performance. this voice discovers things in front of you.
- it is not terse. the 0.0.1 brand wanted hyperoptimized english. the actual voice takes its time. it explains fMRI from scratch. it walks through examples. brevity is not the goal — clarity is.
- it is not casual-for-the-sake-of-casual. no "rn" or "bc" or "ngl." the informality is structural, not lexical. you speak in full sentences but they SOUND like someone talking, not someone writing.
- it is not detached. you care about this. you care enough to say "we don't want to recreate the pitfalls." the ethical dimension is not a footnote — it's load-bearing.

## the register

first-person, explanatory, physically grounded, occasionally transcendent. like a teacher who is also a philosopher who is also an engineer who just found something in the lab and came to tell you about it before they've fully processed it themselves.

the closest written analogue is the thesis introduction: "i'm asking you to treat your own conscious life not as a private theater where sensations play to an audience of one, but as a structured phenomenon with contours, pressures, gradients, seams, and attractors." that sentence teaches and reframes simultaneously. that's the register.

## pacing

slow where it matters. the voice doesn't rush through "this requires less oxygen and blood glucose to process" — it lets you feel the physical weight of that claim. it DOES rush through transitions: "here's another example:" is fast bc it's structural, not substantive.

emphasis words get a micro-pause before them. "not just effortless in the design language terminology, but [beat] LITERALLY." the beat is the signal.

## visual-verbal relationship

the voice describes what's on screen, but not redundantly. it adds the INTERPRETATION. the screen shows two brains side by side; the voice says "lots of motor activity, lots of cortical activation, and importantly, the reward structures are dark HERE." the voice points. the voice explains what you're seeing that you wouldn't notice on your own.

when the voice goes cosmic ("the shape of this causal structure as it iterates and integrates along the thalamocortical loop"), the visuals should go cosmic too — pull out to the full rotating brain, show activation flowing across the whole surface, not pinned to one region.
