# brain-optimized UX — video outline

## the idea

"i built a system that reads your brain's response to UI design" — indie researcher energy. meta dropped a brain model, i used it to see what interfaces actually do to cognition, and the answer changes what good design even means.

## emotional arc

curiosity → understanding → unease → conviction → agency

the viewer should leave feeling like they just got a new lens. not "that was cool" but "i can't unsee this."

## narrative structure

1. **NEWS** — the instrument arrived
2. **BACKGROUND** — what fMRI is, what the brain is doing, why this model matters
3. **THE UNLOCK** — predict brain response → optimize for it
   - 3.1 cluttered vs beautiful UX (metabolic cost, not just aesthetics)
   - 3.2 predictability vs unpredictability (cheap for brain vs taxing)
4. **THE DEEPER POINT** — it's not about one region, it's the trajectory. consciousness as maintenance. preempt the "manipulation" objection.
5. **WHAT I BUILT** — HIX signals, closed loop, trust constraint
6. **CLOSE** — open source, public capability, "violent to cognition"

## open questions

- 60s or 90s? 90 gives the thesis beat room to breathe. 60 forces compression of 3.2 + 4.
- dark patterns: include as a beat within section 3, or cut? powerful but might pull focus from the positive "respect cognition" framing
- section 4 depth: one sentence or a full paragraph of thesis energy?
- which UX pair for 3.2: progressive disclosure vs settings wall? onboarding vs info dump?
- do we show the phone-in-hand screen recording or keep it abstract mockups?
