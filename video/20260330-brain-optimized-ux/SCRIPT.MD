## the script

[chair i]
So Meta recently released v2 of their fMRI scan prediction neural network TRIBE. 
[up-close face]
fMRIs are these scans that show where in the brain blood is moving around and so the TRIBEv2 neural network predicts how 20,000 different will be activated by visiual, audio, and text stimulii. 

, and what it does is predict what 20,000 points on your cortex would look like in a brain scanner — given ANY input. text, audio, video. no human in the loop. just... simulated neuroscience.

**[cut to screen: terminal running, brain images generating]**

so obviously i immediately started feeding it UI descriptions. like, what does a clean google-style search page do to the brain vs a page covered in popups, auto-playing video, flashing banners, cookie walls, the whole nightmare.

**[b-roll: clean UI mockup | cluttered UI mockup — side by side]**

and the answer is: completely different brains. the cluttered version JACKS your frontal cortex. your brain is working overtime just to find the content. it's not engagement — it's struggle.

**[b-roll: paired brain heatmaps, then diff brain]**

but here's where it gets actually disturbing.

**[face to camera, closer]**

i tested dark checkout patterns — you know, the pre-checked insurance, the "you're about to miss out" copy, hidden fees revealed at the last step — and compared them to an honest checkout. three items, clear price, pay now.

**[b-roll: honest checkout mockup | dark checkout mockup]**

the dark version FLIPS your brain's reward signal positive. like, your orbital frontal cortex — the part that goes "ooh nice" — it lights up MORE for the manipulative version. your brain is literally rewarding you for being tricked. that's the mechanism. that's WHY dark patterns work even when you know they're dark patterns.

**[b-roll: rotating brain diff GIF, red hotspots on reward regions]**

**[face to camera]**

and then i found that flow-breaking UI — constant interruptions, save dialogs every 2 minutes, slow autocomplete — it doubles your cognitive load. but here's the trap: engagement metrics go UP too. so if you're optimizing for engagement, you converge on dark patterns. the metric lies.

**[b-roll: flow editor vs interrupted editor, brain comparison]**

so i did something about it. i derived eight brain-based signals from the data — cognitive load, frustration, flow, trust, reward, confusion, engagement, spatial clarity. i'm calling them HIX signals. human interface experience.

**[b-roll: HIX dashboard heatmap]**

the idea is: these become the reward function for autonomous UI design. not "did users click" but "did users' brains THRIVE." and you don't need real users in the loop — the brain model IS the virtual user.

**[b-roll: closed-loop diagram — generator → TRIBE v2 → HIX signals → gradient back]**

but you need trust as a HARD constraint. bc if you optimize purely for reward, you converge on dark patterns. the brain rewards manipulation. you have to constrain the optimization to never decrease trust. that's the ethics built into the math.

**[face to camera, direct]**

all the code is open source. every experiment, every visualization, the whole pipeline. bc i think being able to see what design does to brains should be a public capability, not a competitive moat.

bad UX isn't just annoying. it is measurably violent to cognition. and now we can see it.

**[hold on face for 1 beat, then cut to black + github link]**
